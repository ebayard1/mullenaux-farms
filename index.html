Regenerative Farming, Explained

Farming that gives back more than it takes.

Conventional land use slowly wears soil out. Regenerative farming does the opposite — it rebuilds the land while it produces. Here's what that means, and why it's good news for anyone who owns ground.

Healthy soil is alive. It's a living web of roots, microbes, and organic matter that holds water, feeds plants, and stores carbon. Regenerative farming works with that life instead of against it — and on your land, that turns into real, lasting value.

What is regenerative farming?

Regenerative farming is a set of practices that improve the land over time rather than depleting it. Instead of stripping nutrients and leaving soil bare, it uses natural cycles — grazing animals, rest periods, and ground cover — to rebuild fertility season after season.

At Mullenaux Farms, our tool is the pastured hen. Moved across your land in managed rotations, our flocks do the work nature designed them for: clearing weeds and pests, spreading natural fertilizer, and stirring the soil — then moving on so the ground can rest and rebound.

On your land, step by step

How the cycle actually runs

1 · Managed rotation

Hens live in movable shelters with electric netting. We shift them across your acreage in planned sections so no patch is ever overworked.

2 · Graze, scratch, fertilize

As they roam, hens eat weeds, seeds, and insects, and leave natural fertilizer evenly across the ground.

3 · Rest & regrow

Each section then rests for weeks — long enough for grass to recover stronger and roots to grow deeper than before.

4 · Repeat, and compound

Over seasons, the soil gets richer, the pasture thicker, and your land more productive — a compounding return.
